                                                                      The Payment Authentication use case enables the authentication of payment requests through EU Dig-
                                                                      ital Identity Wallets (EUDI Wallets) for both online and in-store transactions. It can leverage existing
                                                                      payment infrastructures such as SEPA Credit Transfer (SCT), including instant and traditional account-to-account
                                                                      payments, and card payments, complementing current card-based and A2A systems without causing
                                                                      disruption. For online transactions, this integration model supports enriched checkouts where identity
                                                                      attributes (e.g. age) or attestations (e.g. driving license) can be securely shared as part of the payment flow,
                                                                      enabling use cases such as age verification when paying online for an age-restricted service, or presenting
                                                                      a driving license when paying for renting a car online.

                                                                      With regard to authentication for electronic payments, EU Digital Identity users will be able to perform
                                                                      Strong Customer Authentication by using their EU Digital Identity Wallets, ensuring a high level of security
                                                                      and compliance with PSD2 (and with the future PSD3/PSR). In particular, the Wallet must enable the user
                                                                      to give his/her consent for a payment by authenticating transactional information (e.g., payment and payee

Data model, format and proof mechanisms
The Payment Authentication process leverages a Verifiable Credential that proves the user’s identity
(if required) and payment credentials and, where applicable, additional attributes such as age, license status,
or residence. These credentials are in a form of a verifiable attestations, formatted as SD-JWT Verifiable
Credentials or other in the future.

In the context of payments, attestations are often presented to fulfil SCA requirements defined under PSD2,
with wallet-based authentication serving as the “possession” and biometric/PIN acting as the “inherence”
or “knowledge” factor. Selective disclosure is supported, allowing users to share only the required attributes
for a specific payment context, without revealing full identity unnecessarily.

Each of the flows may provide with its own “schema” for transaction processing. Each schema contains (at
least) the following data structures:
1. transactional data schema (for constructing a presentation request),
2. display schema (for rendering information displayed to the Wallet User),
3. localisation information (for proper translations of information for the Wallet User),
4. response schema (for delivering an authentication code as proof of consent for the payment transaction).
