                                                                      In this use case, relying parties are utilizing EUDI Wallets to identify users in proximity scenarios. Relying
                                                                      parties’ objectives may include allowing access to premises to only authorised users, law enforcement,
                                                                      airports or hotels registering the identity of the person they are dealing with, pharmacies checking the
                                                                      identity of a person to see if they have a prescription, shops checking the identity of a person to see if they
                                                                      are authorised to get access to a service or benefits etc.

                                                                      It builds on the same PID, and electronic attestation of attributes framework defined in the European
                                                                      Digital Identity Regulation and the EUDI Wallet Architecture Reference Framework (ARF), but adapts it to
                                                                      short-range, offline-capable communication channels. In order to enable proximity identification, it is
                                                                      expected that the document would need to include a photo, which per PID rulebook1 falls under the portrait
                                                                      attribute in optional attributes specified in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2977 in ARF, however, the
                                                                      mandatory use of photo ID in proximity scenarios remains unclear at the point of writing.
